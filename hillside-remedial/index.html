01 / What hillside remedial means

Engineering work on existing developments.

Hillside remedial works are the engineering services delivered on existing hillside townships, condominiums, and infrastructure where geotechnical elements have failed, deteriorated, or been flagged by post-event review. Different from new-build: the structures, residents, services, and traffic are already there. Works happen around them.

Typical triggers: a slope shows distress (cracks, seepage, settling structures above), a retaining wall has rotated or cracked, drainage has failed and water now ponds on the slope, the local authority has issued a Class III/IV remedial directive, or the management committee has commissioned a condition audit and the engineer has flagged works.

02 / Common scenarios

Where hillside remedial matters most.

Klang Valley hillside townships

Damansara, Mont Kiara, Ampang, Bukit Antarabangsa, Cheras, older hillside developments on weathered granitic and meta-sedimentary slopes where stabilisation systems may need upgrading.

Penang island slopes

Tanjung Bungah, Tanjung Tokong, Air Itam, Penang Hill, post-2017 landslide regulatory tightening drove a programme of remedial directives that continue.

Highland resort and tourism platforms

Genting Highlands, Cameron Highlands, Bukit Tinggi, Fraser's Hill, hill-station hotels, condos, and access roads requiring periodic remedial works under hill-station development guidelines.

Post-landslide programme remediation

State or federal authority-led programmes after major slope failure events, often involves multiple sites with a unified design and procurement framework.

05 / FAQ

Property managers and councils usually ask:

What are hillside remedial works? +
Engineering work to repair, upgrade, or reconstruct slopes, retaining walls, and drainage on existing hillside developments.
Which hillside developments need remedial works? +
Older Klang Valley townships (Damansara, Mont Kiara, Bukit Antarabangsa), Penang island (Tanjung Bungah, Penang Hill), highland resorts (Genting, Cameron Highlands).
What standards apply? +
JKR slope class I-IV (mostly III/IV for hillside remedial), GP categorisation, Hill-Station Development Guidelines, BS 6031, BS 8006, BS 8081, Eurocode 7.
Class III/IV slope grade? +
Yes. CIDB G7 (highest grade), required under most state regulatory regimes for high-consequence hillside slopes.
How do projects proceed? +
Condition assessment → stability analysis & design → phased works (drainage first, then structural) → post-completion monitoring.
