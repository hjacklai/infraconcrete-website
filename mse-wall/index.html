01 / What it is

A composite gravity wall, built from layered fill.

An MSE wall is a Mechanically Stabilized Earth retaining structure where layers of compacted granular fill are reinforced with steel strips, geogrids, or geotextiles, and faced with precast concrete panels, modular concrete blocks, or wire-mesh wraps. The reinforced soil mass behaves as a coherent gravity wall, resisting horizontal earth pressure, surcharge, and external loads through its own weight and the friction between fill and reinforcement.

RE wall (Reinforced Earth wall) and MSE wall are the same family. Both terms describe the same engineering principle, with "RE wall" historically referring to the licensed Reinforced Earth Company panel system and "MSE wall" referring to the broader generic class. Infraconcrete delivers both.

02 / When to choose it

Four scenarios where MSE walls win.

Tall fills and embankments

Embankments and platforms 5–25 m+ tall, where an RC cantilever wall would need disproportionate foundation work. MSE walls scale efficiently with height, and carry heavy surcharge loads on the platform above.

Highway abutments and bridge approaches

Approach embankments to bridges, viaducts, and grade-separated junctions. Precast panel facing gives a clean, predictable architectural face that ties into the bridge structure.

Industrial platform creation

Manufacturing platforms, logistics yards, and warehouse foundations on sloped ground. MSE walls turn cut-and-fill terrain into level developable land at a fraction of the cost of cantilever retaining systems.

Tight schedules and fast track

MSE walls mobilize quickly, once panels are cast and fill is on site, erection is rapid. A 1,000 m² face wall typically runs 6–10 weeks. RC walls take far longer for the same height.

03 / The method

Five stages, delivered in-house.

01

Site assessment and design coordination

Walk the site. Review borehole logs, soil parameters, foundation conditions, drainage. Coordinate with the appointed C&S or geotechnical consultant on assumptions, target factor of safety, exposure class, and standards (BS 8006, FHWA-NHI-10-024, Eurocode 7, JKR). Confirm reinforcement type (steel strip vs geogrid) and facing system.

02

Foundation preparation and levelling pad

Strip topsoil, prepare the foundation to bearing capacity, and cast the unreinforced concrete levelling pad that the bottom row of panels sits on. Survey control is set here, the wall's verticality and alignment depend on the levelling pad accuracy.

03

Panel erection and reinforcement placement

Precast facing panels (or modular blocks) are erected in courses. Steel strips or geogrids are connected to the panel back-face and laid horizontally into the fill at design lengths and vertical spacing (typically 600–800 mm vertical centres for steel strip; 400–600 mm for geogrid).

04

Backfill and compaction in lifts

Granular reinforced fill is placed and compacted in 250–400 mm lifts to the design density (typically 95–98% modified Proctor). The reinforcement is sandwiched between lifts. Compaction equipment near the panel face is restricted to light vibratory plates to avoid panel displacement.

05

Cap, drainage, and verification

Cap beam, parapet, drainage chimney, and weep holes installed per the design. As-built survey verifies wall verticality, alignment, and panel joint widths. Settlement monitoring (where required) is set up for the post-construction period.

06 / Common Questions

Frequently asked.

What is an MSE wall? +
A composite retaining structure where layers of compacted granular fill are reinforced with steel strips, geogrids, or geotextiles, and faced with precast concrete panels or modular blocks. The reinforced soil mass behaves as a coherent gravity wall. RE wall and MSE wall are the same family.
When is MSE the right choice over an RC wall? +
Tall heights (5–25 m+), tight schedules, large fills, and where flexible foundation behaviour is acceptable. They use locally available granular fill, mobilize fast, and tolerate moderate differential settlement. RC walls suit short walls (<5 m) or where foundation must be rigid.
What standards do you design to? +
BS 8006, FHWA-NHI-10-024, BS EN 1997 (Eurocode 7), and JKR Standard Specifications. Project spec governs final detailing.
What facing options? +
Precast concrete panels (classic Reinforced Earth look), modular concrete blocks, wire-mesh wraps with vegetated face, and shotcrete on welded mesh. Selection follows aesthetics, exposure class, and panel turnaround.
Steel strips or geogrids? +
Both, selected per design life, exposure class, and backfill chemistry. Steel strips give predictable stiffness and full traceability. Geogrids suit certain block-faced typologies and aggressive backfills.
How long to build? +
A 1,000 m² face wall on residential platform: 6–10 weeks. Highway-scale 5,000–15,000 m² face walls: 3–6 months. Pace set by panel turnaround and backfill compaction lifts.
Design-build or only construction? +
Both. Consulting engineers engage us as specialist contractor; developers and authorities also appoint us directly under design-build. We prefer involvement from concept rather than tender.
