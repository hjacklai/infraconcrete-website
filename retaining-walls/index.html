01 / Wall types we deliver

Five families, one specialist team.

Reinforced Earth (RE / MSE) walls

Geogrid or steel-strip · BS 8006 / FHWA

Geosynthetic or metallic reinforcement strips/grids embedded in compacted backfill behind facing panels or modular blocks. Most economical above 4–5 m. Tolerates settlement. Common on highway corridors, hillside developments, and mass-fill platforms.

Modular block walls (segmental retaining walls)

Dry-stacked precast · BS 8002 / NCMA

Precast concrete blocks dry-stacked with mechanical interlock. Up to ~8–10 m unreinforced; higher with geogrid. Fast install, textured/coloured aesthetic finishes available. Common on residential, township, and parks.

RC cantilever and counterfort walls

Stiff, low settlement · BS 8002 / Eurocode 7

Cast-in-place reinforced concrete cantilever walls, economical to ~6 m, with counterforts above. Used where settlement tolerance is low, backfill space is constrained, or aesthetic/exposure dictates monolithic concrete.

Sheet piling and soldier piling

Driven or vibrated · BS EN 12063 / BS 8002

Steel sheet piles for excavation support, basement temp works, riverside, marine, and certain permanent retaining applications. Soldier piles with timber or concrete lagging for granular soils. We size depth, profile, and tieback design to the load case.

Gabion and crib walls

Stone-filled mesh · BS 1052 / BS EN 10223

Wire-mesh baskets stone-filled, stacked to form gravity walls. Permeable, flexible, naturalistic. Suited to riverbanks, low retaining heights, and erosion-prone sites where rigidity isn't required.

Combined and hybrid systems

When one wall isn't enough

Higher walls often combine: RE wall on top of an RC base, or sheet piling with a tied-back upper modular section. We design and build the whole system rather than coordinating between specialist trades.

02 / When to use each

Picking the right wall for the right site.

Hillside developments & townships

RE walls and modular block, economical at scale, faster install, accommodate settlement on filled platforms.

Highway corridors and embankments

RE walls and RC cantilever for height and load. Fascia/aesthetic options for visible faces. Coordination with traffic management for live works.

Basement and excavation support

Sheet piling and soldier piling for temporary works. Sometimes secant/contiguous bored pile walls for permanent basement walls.

Riverside, marine, naturalistic

Gabion and crib walls, permeable, flexible, blend into landscape. Or coated steel sheet piling where structural capacity is needed.

06 / FAQ

What engineers usually ask first.

What types of retaining walls do you build? +
RE / MSE walls (geogrid or steel strip), modular concrete block walls, RC cantilever walls, sheet piling and soldier piling, gabion walls. Plus hybrid systems combining two or more.
RE wall vs RC wall, which? +
RE walls usually cheaper above 4–5 m, accommodate settlement, fast install. RC cantilever stiffer, used when settlement tolerance is low or backfill space is constrained. We size both early in design.
What standards? +
BS 8002, BS 8004, BS EN 1997 (Eurocode 7), BS 8006, JKR specifications. Coordinated with the appointed C&S or geotechnical consultant.
How tall can the wall be? +
Modular block 8–10 m unreinforced, higher with geogrid. RE walls 25 m+ in Malaysia. RC cantilever 6 m+ with counterforts. Sheet piling design-led by load and soil.
Drainage behind the wall? +
Yes, and not optional. Granular drainage zones, filter geotextiles, perforated drains, weepholes, all designed and built as part of the wall scope.
Sheet piling temporary or permanent? +
Both. Temporary for basement / excavation support with extraction. Permanent for marine, riverside, certain retaining applications, with corrosion protection sized to design life.
Hillside or unstable ground? +
Yes. We deliver soil nailing, micropiles, ground improvement, and the wall as one coordinated package.
Aesthetic finishes? +
Modular blocks with textured / coloured finishes. RE wall facing panels patterned, fair-faced, or vegetated. RC walls with stone or formliner. Specify with the design.
Programme for a typical wall? +
A 50 m run of 6 m-high modular wall typically 6–10 weeks including drainage and backfill. RE walls similar. Sheet piling can be faster.
What does it cost? +
Per m² of face by wall type, plus foundations, drainage, backfill. Costs vary 4-5x between types. Send geometry on WhatsApp for a same-day budget.

Have ground that needs holding back?

Send the wall geometry, ground conditions, and aesthetic / settlement requirements. Same-day response with the wall type that fits and a budget range.
