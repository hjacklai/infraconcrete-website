01 / What it is

Steel mesh that holds the rock face together.

Rock netting is a high-tensile steel mesh anchored to or draped over a rock face. Its job is to intercept loose rock and prevent rockfall reaching the asset below — a road, rail line, building, or infrastructure corridor.

There are two main families:

Drape mesh (passive system): Double-twisted hexagonal wire mesh hung from the crest of the rock face and allowed to hang loose. Rock that detaches from the face is funnelled down behind the mesh to a catchment ditch at the toe. Simple, robust, monsoon-tolerant. The classic choice for highway cut faces.

TECCO-class high-tensile mesh (active system): A diamond-pattern steel mesh installed under tension and anchored at a regular grid pattern. The mesh applies a clamping force to the rock face, holding loose blocks in place rather than catching them after they detach. Higher performance for active stabilization.

02 / When to choose it

Four scenarios where rock netting is the answer.

Cut rock faces on highway corridors

The most common use. Cut rock alongside highways degrades over time as rainfall and freeze-thaw open joints. Rock netting catches debris before it reaches the carriageway, protecting traffic and avoiding emergency closures.

Cliffs above access roads and infrastructure

Natural cliffs above road, rail, building, or substation. Rock netting transforms the cliff face from an unmanaged hazard into an engineered, monitored system with a defined factor of safety.

Mining benches and quarry walls

Active and decommissioned mining benches where rockfall threatens haul roads, plant, or workers below. Rock netting is faster and lower-disruption than full bench reshaping.

Tunnel portals and rock-cut starts

Rock above tunnel portals and cuttings where rock bolting alone leaves the face vulnerable to small-debris fall. Netting catches what bolts can't pin.

03 / The method

Five stages, delivered in-house.

01

Geological mapping and hazard assessment

Walk the rock face. Map joint sets, weathering grade, kinematic instability modes (planar, wedge, toppling), and rock block sizes. Coordinate with the appointed geotechnical consultant. Decide drape vs TECCO based on hazard class, height, and consequence.

02

Hand-scaling of loose rock

Rope-access crews descend the face and remove obvious loose blocks before mesh installation. This is mandatory — netting is a containment system, not a substitute for removing major loose rock first.

03

Crest and toe anchor installation

Crest anchors (typically 3–6 m galvanised soil-nail-class anchors) are drilled into competent rock or stable soil above the face. Toe anchors fix the bottom edge. For TECCO systems, additional grid-pattern face anchors are installed at design spacing.

04

Mesh deployment and tensioning

Mesh rolls are lowered from the crest and laced together at panel overlaps with cable. For drape mesh, the bottom edge is tied off to the toe cable and left loose. For TECCO mesh, face anchors are torqued through the mesh with bearing plates to apply design clamping pressure.

05

Verification and handover

As-built survey of anchor positions and mesh coverage. Pull-out tests on sacrificial anchors per the design spec. Handover with as-built drawings, anchor test certificates, and a maintenance schedule (typically annual visual inspection plus 5-yearly anchor recheck).

06 / Common Questions

Frequently asked.

What is rock netting? +
High-tensile steel mesh anchored to or draped over a rock face to intercept loose rock and prevent rockfall. Two families: drape mesh (passive, hangs from crest) and TECCO-class active mesh (under tension, applies clamping pressure).
How is it different from a rockfall barrier? +
Rockfall barriers are free-standing energy-rated kits placed at a distance from the face — they catch rock in flight. Rock netting is fixed directly to or against the face. The two are often used together: netting on the face, barriers at the road edge.
When is rock netting the right call? +
Cut rock alongside highways and rail. Cliffs above access roads, towns, or infrastructure. Mining benches and quarry walls. Tunnel portals. Often combined with rock bolting: bolts pin major blocks, netting catches smaller debris.
What standards? +
ETAG 027 / EAD 340059 (Falling Rock Protection Kits) and JKR Standard Specifications.
Drape mesh or TECCO mesh? +
Drape mesh: double-twisted hexagonal wire, hung loose, catches falling rock. Simple and robust. TECCO mesh: diamond-pattern high-tensile steel under tension, anchored at a grid — clamps the face. Higher performance, higher cost, used where active stabilization is needed.
How is it installed? +
Crest anchors first. Mesh rolled from crest. For drape: lace at panel overlaps, tie off bottom to toe cable. For TECCO: face anchors drilled through mesh in grid pattern, tensioned with bearing plates. Hand-scaling of obvious loose rock precedes mesh installation.
Can it be done on live highways? +
Yes. Staged closures and lane management approved by the Highway Authority. Rope-access crews work off-peak. EKVE rock netting was delivered without halting traffic.
How long? +
500 m² rock face: 2–4 weeks. Larger 5,000 m²+ highway-scale: 2–4 months. Pace constrained by access (rope-access crews) and weather (monsoon).
