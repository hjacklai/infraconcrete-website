How it works

From years to months.

Without PVD, soft clay consolidates by vertical drainage through the clay itself. For a typical Malaysian marine clay deposit 10-30 m thick, that takes 5-20 years. The structural development above must wait. With PVD at design grid spacing, the drainage path is reduced from tens of metres of clay to a single horizontal metre or less: pore water flows horizontally a short distance to the nearest PVD, then vertically up the PVD core to the surface drainage blanket. Consolidation time drops from years to months (typically 3-12 months depending on clay properties and preload). Combined with surcharge preload, the PVD programme delivers a stable platform ready for permanent works in a single operating season.

Installation sequence

Eight-step process.

  1. Working platform. Granular sand drainage blanket 0.5-1.0 m thick over the soft soil. Supports the installation rig and acts as horizontal drainage layer for PVD discharge.
  2. PVD set-out. Design grid (1.0-2.0 m triangular or square) marked across the site.
  3. Rig deployment. Crawler-mounted mast rig with mandrel; PVD spool feed.
  4. Mandrel insertion. PVD threaded through mandrel; mandrel driven vertically into soft clay to design depth (10-30 m); PVD anchored at the base with anchor plate.
  5. Mandrel withdrawal. Mandrel withdrawn leaving PVD in place.
  6. Cut-off. PVD cut at sand blanket level for connection to horizontal drainage.
  7. Repeat across grid. Production rate 1,000-3,000 m of PVD per rig per shift typical.
  8. Surcharge preload + monitoring. Sand or rock fill to design preload elevation; settlement monitored via settlement plates and pneumatic piezometers; preload removed when target consolidation degree achieved.
