01 / What it is

Pinning loose rock back into competent strata.

Rock bolting installs tensioned or fully-grouted high-yield steel bolts that anchor unstable rock blocks into the sound rock mass behind them. With bearing plates at the face, the bolts transfer load from the loose blocks into the surrounding stable rock, the face is then secured in place rather than allowed to ravel or release.

It is the right call when a cut rock face shows block instability, when a tunnel portal needs immediate stabilization on excavation, or when a quarry bench has weathered veneer over competent rock that must hold for the design life.

02 / When to choose it

Four scenarios where rock bolting wins.

Cut rock faces with block instability

Highway and infrastructure cuts where joint sets and bedding planes daylight on the face. Rock bolts pin the wedges and toppling blocks before progressive ravelling begins.

Tunnel portals and intersections

Stabilization at portals and shotcrete-supported tunnel intersections during excavation. Often combined with rock netting and shotcrete.

Quarry benches and high rock walls

Long-term stabilization of quarry faces and high rock cuts where back-break, weathering, and seismic loading need to be managed across design life.

Weathered rock veneer over sound rock

Slopes with a thin layer of weathered or fractured rock over competent rock behind. Bolts transfer load through the weak veneer into the strong substrate.

03 / The method

Five stages, delivered in-house.

01

Geological mapping and design coordination

Map joint sets, bedding, weathering depth, and groundwater. Coordinate with the appointed geotechnical or C&S consultant on bolt pattern, capacity, length, corrosion-protection class, and standards (BS 8081, EN 1537, JKR spec).

02

Drilling to engineered depth

Hydraulic or pneumatic rigs install holes at the design diameter (typically 45–110 mm) and length (3–12 m), oriented to intercept the joint sets at the right angle. Hole positions follow the design grid.

03

Bolt installation and anchoring

High-yield bar (Y25–Y32) is installed with centralizers. Anchorage is by mechanical end-anchor, resin cartridge, or full-column cement grout, selected per the design and rock conditions.

04

Tensioning and lock-off

For tensioned anchors, lock-off is to the design proof load, typically 60–80% of bar yield. Bearing plates seat the load to the face. Untensioned grouted bolts are tightened to seating after cure.

05

Acceptance testing and inspection

Pre-production sacrificial bolt pull-out tests, then proof tests on a percentage of production bolts (typically 5–10% per BS 8081). Lift-off tests on tensioned anchors. Reports submitted to the consultant before next-stage works.

07 / FAQ

What engineers usually ask first.

What is rock bolting? +
Tensioned or fully-grouted high-yield steel bolts drilled into rock to anchor loose or jointed blocks into the competent rock mass behind. Bearing plates at the face transfer load from the unstable blocks into the stable rock.
Tensioned, untensioned, mechanical, resin, or grouted? +
Choice depends on rock quality, function, and design life. Mechanical end-anchors give immediate tensioning. Resin cartridges set fast. Cement grout gives the best long-term corrosion protection, standard for permanent works.
What standards do you design and install to? +
BS 8081, EN 1537, AASHTO, and JKR specifications. Designs coordinated with the appointed geotechnical or C&S consultant.
Typical bar diameter, length, and capacity? +
Y25–Y32 deformed steel, hole 45–110 mm, length 3–12 m, working load 100–400 kN per bolt, lock-off 60–80% of yield for tensioned anchors. Final values per design.
Live traffic or near occupied buildings? +
Yes. EKVE rock-face works are an example, delivered without halting traffic.
Tunnel-portal rock bolting? +
Yes, both portal stabilization and surrounding rock cuts. Often combined with rock netting, shotcrete, and dewatering.
How is acceptance tested? +
Pre-production sacrificial pull-out tests, then proof tests on 5–10% of production bolts per BS 8081. Lift-off tests on tensioned anchors.
Is corrosion protection included? +
Yes. Single (SCP) or double corrosion protection (DCP) per BS 8081, sized to design life and aggressivity.
How does it combine with other slope works? +
Almost always combined: bolts + mesh + shotcrete + horizontal drains, plus rock netting / rockfall barriers if rockfall hazard is high. All delivered in-house.
What does it cost? +
Quoted per bolt with separate rates for drilling, supply, testing, and head detailing. Send rough geometry on WhatsApp for a same-day budget estimate.
