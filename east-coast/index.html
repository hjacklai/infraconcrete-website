01 / East Coast slope context

Heaviest monsoon, most demanding drainage.

The East Coast's annual monsoon (November-February) drives the highest rainfall in Peninsular Malaysia. Slopes, embankments, and earthworks built for west-coast assumptions fail recurrently. The Cameron Highlands–Gua Musang–Kuala Krai corridor through the Titiwangsa Range sees regular landslide events. The coastal road through Terengganu and Kelantan sees flood-driven scour and erosion.

Three project patterns dominate:

ECRL and East Coast corridor. The East Coast Rail Link delivered slope protection across 64 km of chainage with 320,000 m of drilled drains and soil nails — that's the reference project. Plus East Coast Expressway and federal-route slope maintenance.

Hill-corridor remediation through Titiwangsa. Kuala Krai, Gua Musang, Tanah Merah — recurrent monsoon-driven slope failures requiring emergency response and durable remediation.

Coastal and riverside protection. Sheet piling, retaining walls, and erosion control for the long coastline and tidal river systems through Kemaman, Dungun, Kuala Terengganu.
