01 / What it is

Reinforcing slopes with high-strength steel.

Soil nailing is a slope reinforcement technique. We drill into the slope at engineered angles, insert high-yield steel bars, and grout them in place. The reinforced soil mass behaves as a coherent block, it resists sliding, rotational failure, and progressive distress without major excavation or full retaining wall reconstruction.

It is the right call when the existing ground must stay where it is, when traffic, structures, or schedule won't tolerate full removal, and you still need a slope that holds for design life.

02 / When to choose it

Four scenarios where soil nailing wins.

Cut slopes on highway and infrastructure

New cut faces along highways, railway corridors, and access roads. Soil nailing locks the cut against progressive failure as weathering and rainfall act on it.

Retained excavations on tight sites

Basement and platform excavations adjacent to property lines or live structures, where there's no room for battered slopes or full retaining systems.

Distressed or failing existing slopes

Slopes showing cracks, seepage, settling structures above, or prior landslide history. Remedial soil nailing arrests movement and re-establishes factor of safety.

Hillside developments and platform creation

Township and mixed-development cut slopes where land value depends on stable, buildable platforms above and below the engineered face.

03 / The method

Five stages, delivered in-house.

01

Site assessment and design coordination

Walk the slope. Review borehole logs, soil parameters, slope geometry, drainage. Coordinate with the appointed C&S or geotechnical consultant on assumptions, target factor of safety, and standards (BS 8006-2, FHWA-NHI-14-007, Eurocode 7, JKR spec).

02

Drilling at engineered angles

Drilling rigs install holes at the design diameter (typically 100–150 mm), length (6–15 m), and inclination (10–15° below horizontal). Hole positions follow the design grid, typically 1.5–2.5 m horizontal and vertical centres.

03

Nail installation and grouting

High-yield steel bars (Y20–Y32) are installed with centralizers and tremied with cement grout to a minimum 30 N/mm² strength. Grouting under pressure where the design specifies it. Bond length and free length set per the design.

04

Face application, guniting / shotcrete

Welded steel mesh is fixed across the face with bearing plates onto each nail head. Shotcrete is sprayed over the mesh in lifts to the design thickness, finished with weep pipes and chute drains. Vegetated mats over mesh are an alternative for environmentally sensitive sites.

05

Load testing and verification

Pull-out tests on sacrificial nails per BS 8006 / FHWA. Results back to the consultant before production nails proceed. Final acceptance per the project's testing regime, typically 5% of installed nails for verification testing.

07 / FAQ

What engineers usually ask first.

What is soil nailing? +
A slope reinforcement technique. High-strength steel bars are drilled into the soil at engineered angles, grouted in place, and the face is finished with shotcrete or facing panels. The reinforced soil mass behaves as a coherent block that resists sliding and rotational failure.
When is soil nailing the right call? +
Cut slopes, retained excavations, and existing slopes showing distress, especially where major excavation or full retaining wall reconstruction is not practical. It works on cohesive and granular soils, weathered rock, and is often combined with guniting, rock bolting, and horizontal drains.
What standards do you design and install to? +
Designs typically reference BS 8006-2, FHWA-NHI-14-007 (Soil Nail Walls Reference Manual), Eurocode 7, and JKR specifications for highway and government works. We coordinate with the appointed C&S or geotechnical consultant on parameters and submit method statements per the contract spec.
How long does it take on a typical site? +
Site-dependent. A 1,000 m² residential cut slope typically takes 4–6 weeks from mobilization to handover. A 20,000 m²+ government slope programme runs 6–12 months. Mobilization in emergency conditions can be days. A detailed programme is attached to every proposal.
Do you do design-build or only construction? +
Both. Many consulting engineers and C&S consultants engage us as the specialist contractor under their design. Developers and authorities also appoint us directly under design-build, where we lead the geotechnical design and construction. We prefer to be involved from concept rather than tender.
Typical bar diameter, length, and spacing? +
Common ranges (verified per design): bar diameter Y20–Y32 high-yield steel, hole diameter 100–150 mm, length 6–15 m depending on slope geometry and soil parameters, spacing 1.5–2.5 m horizontally and vertically, grout cementitious to 30 N/mm² minimum, inclination 10–15° below horizontal. Final values come from the design analysis.
Can soil nailing be done under live traffic? +
Yes. We deliver soil nailing on live highway corridors using staged closures and lane management approved by the Highway Authority. EKVE and ECRL are examples, both delivered without halting traffic.
How is the face finished? +
Most commonly with shotcrete (guniting) reinforced with welded mesh, finished with weep pipes and chute drains. For environmentally sensitive sites we use vegetated erosion mats over the mesh. Face design follows the design engineer's specification.
Is load testing included? +
Yes. We perform pull-out tests on sacrificial nails per BS 8006 / FHWA recommendations to verify bond capacity and design assumptions. Results are reported back to the consultant before production nails proceed.
What does soil nailing cost? +
Varies by site geometry, soil conditions, and finishing. We quote per m² of finished face plus per-metre nail rates after site assessment. For budget estimates ahead of consultant engagement, share rough slope geometry on WhatsApp and we'll respond same day.
