01 / How geocell works

Confine the fill, borrow its stiffness.

Granular fill on its own resists vertical load by friction and minimal cohesion. Under cyclic traffic or rainfall the grains migrate, the surface ravels, and bearing capacity drops. Confining the same fill inside a honeycomb of welded HDPE strips changes the mechanics: each cell wall provides hoop tension that resists outward spread, and the welded seams provide interlock between cells. Confined fill is now a composite layer — measurably stiffer (typically 2-3× modulus) than the same fill without confinement.

This confinement effect is the basis for all four StrataWeb application families: slope protection, channel lining, load support on weak subgrade, and retaining-wall infill. Same product, four applications, design varies by cell depth and fill type.

02 / Four applications

Where StrataWeb fits.

Vegetated slope protection

Cell depth 75-100 mm. Open cells filled with topsoil + Vetiver / local grass species. Holds soil on slopes 1V:1.5H to 1V:0.7H through Malaysian monsoon. Perforated walls let vegetation roots interlock between cells. Common on highway and rail cut slopes, hillside developments.

Concrete-filled channel lining

Cell depth 100-150 mm. Cells filled with C25-C30 concrete become an articulated channel lining, flexes with differential settlement, no joints to leak. Replaces cast-in-situ concrete channel. High-velocity (>5 m/s) monsoon discharge channels, spillways, drainage outfalls.

Load support on weak subgrade

Cell depth 100-200 mm. Aggregate-filled cells over a separation geotextile reduce aggregate thickness 30-50% vs unconfined. CBR equivalence — 300 mm geocell-confined ≈ 600 mm conventional on soft clay. Mining haul, plantation access, lay-down yards, parking platforms.

Retaining wall & gravity wall

Stacked geocell layers, soil-filled, become a gravity retaining wall. Vegetatable face. Lower-cost alternative to gabion in 1-4 m height range. Combined with StrataGrid for taller walls. Architectural finish — green slope, no exposed steel.

05 / Installation method

How StrataWeb goes in.

01

Subgrade preparation

Strip topsoil, grade to design profile, place separation geotextile (nonwoven 200-300 g/m²) on weak subgrade. Subgrade compaction to 95% MDD where applicable.

02

Anchor & open the panel

Pin top of slope or top edge of installation with steel J-pins or proprietary anchors. Open the welded panel — cells expand to design dimensions. Verify cell shape via diagonal measurement.

03

Tendon connection (slopes)

For steep slopes, connect adjacent panels via woven polyester tendons through factory-formed slots. Anchor tendons to top-of-slope deadman trench or rock bolts. Establishes panel-to-panel continuity.

04

Fill the cells

Place fill from the lowest point upward (slope) or from one end (channel). Avoid dropping fill from height — risks bursting cell walls. Compact lightly via hand or vibratory plate. Top off cells flush with cell walls.

05

Vegetation / surface treatment

For vegetated applications: hydroseed Vetiver / local grass mix. Cover with coir mat for first monsoon. Inspect after first 3 months; reseed bare cells. For concrete fill: cure 7-14 days, no traffic until C25 strength reached.

Where StrataWeb is at home.

Highway cut slopes

07 / FAQ

Engineers and developers usually ask:

What's the cell depth for my application? +
75-100 mm vegetated slopes, 100-150 mm channel lining + light load, 150-200 mm heavy load + retaining walls.
Steep slope limit? +
Vegetated 1V:1H. Aggregate / concrete fill 1V:0.5H+. Anchored top-of-slope tendons required >1V:1H.
Concrete or aggregate fill in channels? +
Concrete for >5 m/s flow / permanent monsoon channels. Aggregate for moderate flow.
Load support saving? +
30-50% aggregate thickness reduction over weak subgrade. Soft clay 600 mm conventional ≈ 300 mm geocell + 150 mm geocell.
Standards? +
ASTM D7864, ISO 13426, NCMA, FHWA-NHI-15-067, AASHTO LRFD, BS EN 13252, JKR.
Lead time? +
Common depths/perforations ex-stock or 1-3 weeks. Specialised dimensions 4-8 weeks ex-factory.
