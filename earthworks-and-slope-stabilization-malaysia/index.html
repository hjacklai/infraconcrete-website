01 / Why one package

Why bundle earthworks with slope works under one specialist.

On flat land, earthworks and slope stabilization can be separate contracts without much harm. On a hillside development they cannot. The batter you cut a slope to, the compaction of the fill behind a wall, and the drainage set into a platform are the same decisions that decide whether the slope moves. Splitting them across two contractors builds a boundary into the works, and that boundary is exactly where distress appears. Here is what changes when one CIDB G7 contractor carries both.

No interface gap, no finger-pointing

When earthworks and slope works sit in separate contracts, a moving slope or a cracked wall triggers a dispute over whose scope failed. Under one contract there is one responsible party for the cut, the fill, the wall and the drainage. One method statement, one programme, one liability.

Cut trimmed to the design batter

Soil nail and shotcrete designs assume a specific cut face angle and bench geometry. When the same crew cuts and stabilizes, the excavated face is trimmed to the batter the design actually assumes, not left over-steep for a later specialist to correct or under-cut and re-profiled at cost.

Backfill placed to the wall design

Retaining and reinforced-earth walls are only as good as the structural backfill behind them. One contractor places fill in 200 to 300 mm lifts, tests to 98 percent MDD, and loads the wall against tested fill. No handover of a wall built against fill someone else placed to an unknown standard.

Drainage built in, not retrofitted

Horizontal drains, weepholes, cascade drains and subsoil drains are set as the fill and walls rise. Groundwater is the single most common failure driver on Malaysian slopes; building drainage into the sequence is far cheaper and more reliable than coring drains into a distressed slope after the event.

One programme, monsoon-aware

Open cut faces left through a wet season are a liability. A single programme sequences the cut so faces are stabilized as they are exposed, and coordinates earthworks output with the nailing and facing crews so the site is never carrying a large unstabilized exposure into heavy rain.

One QA chain, one as-built

Compaction records, soil nail test results and drain positions are logged in one ISO 9001:2015 chain. The consultant receives a single coherent as-built rather than two sets of records that disagree at the earthworks-to-slope boundary. Cleaner submissions to JKR, DOE and the local authority.

02 / What the package delivers

One contract, first strip to stabilized slope.

Bulk earthworks, cut and fill

Site clearing and topsoil strip, bulk cut and haul, bulk fill placement and compaction, platform formation and subgrade trim. General fill to 95 percent MDD, structural backfill to 98 percent MDD, in controlled 200 to 300 mm lifts to BS 6031, MS 1227 and JKR-SPJ Section 2.

Cut slope soil nailing

Soil nailing on hillside cut slopes with shotcrete and welded mesh facing to hold the cut face at the design batter. Drilled-and-grouted high-yield bar, full testing programme, to JKR-SPJ Section 7 and the JKR Slope Engineering Manual. See /soil-nailing/.

Retaining and reinforced-earth walls

Reinforced-earth, reinforced-concrete and gravity walls to retain platforms and cut faces, built against tested structural backfill. Reinforced-earth systems available through group company Starwall (sole STRATA Geosystems distributor in Malaysia). See /retaining-walls/.

Horizontal drains and slope drainage

Sub-horizontal drains to relieve pore pressure where groundwater drives instability, plus cascade drains, berm drains, weepholes and subsoil drains built into the platform as it forms. See /horizontal-drains/.

Erosion and sediment control

ESCP-compliant erosion and sediment control: silt traps, sediment basins, turfing, erosion-control matting and slope vegetation to DOE and JPS requirements, coordinated with the earthworks sequence rather than added at the end. See /erosion-control/.

Ground improvement and detention

Where soft ground or high runoff needs it, soil improvement and flood detention ponds are folded into the same package, as delivered at Alam Impian across Precincts 6 to 10. See /ground-improvement/.

07 / FAQ

Developers, consultants and main contractors usually ask:

Why deliver earthworks and slope stabilization as one integrated package? +
On hillside and cut-and-fill sites the earthworks and the slope stabilization are the same problem seen twice. The batter a cut ends up at, the compaction of structural backfill behind a wall, and the drainage set into a platform all decide whether the slope stands. When one CIDB G7 contractor carries both, there is no interface gap: the cut is trimmed to the batter the soil nail design assumes, backfill is placed and tested to the wall design, and drains are built into the fill as it rises rather than retrofitted after failure. Splitting the two contracts builds a boundary into the works, and that boundary is where distress and disputes appear. Infraconcrete delivers cut and fill, soil nailing, shotcrete, retaining walls, horizontal drains and drainage under one contract, one programme and one QA chain.
What CIDB grade and categories cover integrated earthworks and slope works? +
CIDB Grade G7 (highest contractor grade, no upper tender value limit). The categories that matter for an integrated package are all held in-house: CE01 (earthworks), CE08 (slope works), CE21 (geotechnical), plus B04 (specialised building works) and M15 (mechanical). Because CE01, CE08 and CE21 sit under one registration, the bulk earthworks, the retaining structures and the slope stabilization all run under a single G7 contract. PPK Registration 0120220822-WP104845, valid 22 August 2022 to 13 December 2027. ISO 9001:2015 certified.
What scope does the integrated package cover? +
Site clearing and topsoil strip; bulk cut and haul; bulk fill placement and compaction to 95 percent MDD general fill and 98 percent MDD structural backfill; platform formation and subgrade trim; cut slope battering and benching; soil nailing with shotcrete and welded mesh facing; retaining and reinforced-earth walls; horizontal drains; surface drainage, cascade drains, silt traps and erosion control; and flood detention ponds where required. First strip to stabilized, drained, vegetated slope, all under one programme with as-built records per ISO 9001:2015.
Which standards govern the earthworks and the slope works? +
Earthworks to BS 6031, soil testing to BS 1377, ground investigation interpreted per BS 5930, geotechnical design per BS EN 1997 (Eurocode 7), compaction referencing MS 1227. On federal and government works the earthworks follow JKR-SPJ Section 2 and the slope and soil nail scope follows JKR-SPJ Section 7 and the JKR Slope Engineering Manual. One contractor holding both means the earthworks spec and the slope spec are read together, not in isolation.
How do you avoid interface gaps between the earthworks and slope trades? +
A single programme and a single engineer-in-charge. The cut sequence is planned so faces are stabilized as they are exposed rather than left open through a monsoon. Structural backfill behind walls is placed in 200 to 300 mm lifts and density-tested to 98 percent MDD before the next lift, so the wall is never loaded against loose fill. Horizontal drains and weepholes are set as the fill or wall rises, not cored in afterwards. The same QA chain records the compaction, the nail tests and the drain positions, so the consultant receives one coherent as-built rather than two that disagree at the boundary.
What compaction control do you apply behind retaining structures? +
General fill to a minimum 95 percent MDD; structural backfill behind retaining and reinforced-earth walls to a minimum 98 percent MDD. Fill is placed in 200 to 300 mm lifts using smooth-drum vibratory or padfoot rollers, with pneumatic-tyre and plate compaction close to the wall face to avoid over-stressing the facing. Field density is confirmed by nuclear density gauge or sand-replacement, moisture is kept near Proctor optimum, and CBR is checked on the subgrade. Records are logged and issued per ISO 9001:2015 before the wall is loaded.
